# CISA Secure Our World & NIST Human Factors Guidance: Social Engineering Defense

*Source: CISA "Secure Our World" Framework & NIST Human Factors in Cybersecurity Guidelines.*

---

## 1. What Social Engineering Is
- **Social engineering** targets human psychology (trust, fear, urgency, curiosity, or the desire to help) rather than exploiting technical software bugs.
- Attackers manipulate human emotions to trick individuals into divulging sensitive information, clicking malicious links, downloading fraudulent attachments, or granting unauthorized access.
- It spans multiple channels, including email, text messages (smishing), social media direct messages, phone calls (vishing), and in-person impersonation.

---

## 2. Core Manipulation Tactics & Red Flags
- **Artificial Urgency & Fear:** Messages claiming dire consequences, immediate account suspension, or legal trouble if action is not taken instantly.
- **Authority Impersonation:** Posing as IT support, company executives, law enforcement, or trusted government agencies to demand compliance.
- **Too Good to Be True / Flattery ("You're special"):** Enticing offers, prizes, or appeals designed to lower critical thinking and hesitation.
- **Humanitarian Appeals:** Manipulating empathy during crises or urgent disasters.
- **Mismatched Channels:** Unusual requests sent via unverified communication channels or personal accounts for official matters.

---

## 3. CISA "Secure Our World" 3-Step Defense Protocol

1. **Recognize:** 
   - Look for red flags: urgent language, requests for personal or financial data, untrusted links, and mismatched sender details.
   - Note: While bad grammar was once a primary indicator, modern AI tools mean malicious messages may feature flawless spelling and grammar, making behavioral cues even more critical.

2. **Resist:**
   - Resist the temptation to click links, download attachments, or panic-react to unexpected demands.
   - Never share multi-factor authentication (MFA) codes, passwords, or PINs with anyone under any circumstances.

3. **Report & Delete:**
   - Report suspicious messages using official reporting mechanisms or spam/phishing buttons.
   - Delete the message immediately without clicking unsubscribe links or replying.

---

## 4. Verification & Out-of-Band Confirmation
- **Independent Channel Verification:** If you receive a strange request from a colleague, friend, or supervisor (e.g., via chat or email asking for funds or urgent favors), **use an independent, pre-established secondary channel** (such as calling their known phone number directly) to confirm authenticity.
- Never trust the contact information or callback numbers provided inside a suspicious message.

---

## 5. Quick Reference — The Most Important Rules
1. Social engineering exploits human trust and emotions, not software code.
2. Attackers frequently impersonate trusted authorities (IT, executives, support).
3. **Recognize, Resist, and Report** every suspicious communication.
4. **NEVER** share OTP codes, passwords, or credentials.
5. Always verify requests through an independent, pre-established channel.
